Get ready

If you already DKIM-sign, you’re most of the way there. DKIM2 reuses your keys — no new DNS to make it work.

Make alignment solid

When you originate a message, DKIM2 requires three things to line up:

  • the envelope sender (5321.MAIL FROM),
  • the From: header (5322.From),
  • the signing domain (d=).

DMARC through DKIM already needs From and d= aligned; DKIM2 adds the envelope sender. Watch out: if you pass DMARC only through an aligned SPF, that won’t carry over — DKIM2 needs DKIM alignment.

ESPs, bounces and feedback

  • Using an ESP? They sign on your behalf (you publish a key, or a CNAME delegated to them), or they rewrite your From into a domain they control. Most ESP customers need do nothing.
  • Expect more asynchronous bounces. DKIM2 makes bounces safe to send after the connection closes, and the chain routes them home — make sure your bounce processing can handle them.
  • Want handling feedback? Ask for it with the f=feedback flag. The report format is still being defined.

What should your organization do now?

  1. Get DKIM keys first. Only SPF, or nothing at all? Start DKIM-signing with aligned keys now — DKIM2 builds on them.
  2. Pick an owner. Decide who in your organization owns DKIM2.
  3. Map your modifications. Find where your pipeline changes content, rewrites addresses, or expands one message to many recipients — and cut changes you don’t need. Every change you make is one you’ll be accountable for.
  4. Plan your signing workflow. Where you verify incoming signatures, drop invalid ones, add your own, and extend existing chains.
  5. Ask your vendors. MTA, ESP and gateway vendors: what’s their DKIM2 implementation and interop plan? See the software that already supports DKIM2.
